The honest part

Logistic regression models odds, not the probability directly

Logistic regression predicts the probability of a binary event through the log-odds, which keeps predictions between 0 and 1 and lets each predictor's effect be written as an odds ratio. An odds ratio of 2 means the odds of the event double per one-unit increase in the predictor, holding the others fixed. Magic Stat reports each coefficient, its odds ratio and their confidence intervals.

How the outcome is encoded matters. A column of 0/1 is used as is; a two-category text column is figured out automatically, with the second level in order treated as the event. The dialog states which level is the event, so the odds ratios cannot be read backwards.

Logistic regression has a specific failure mode: perfect separation, when a predictor splits the two outcome groups completely. Then the maximum-likelihood estimates do not exist, and software that returns huge coefficients with tiny standard errors is reporting an artefact. Magic Stat refuses to present such estimates and names the predictor responsible.

What it does

What Magic Stat gives you for logistic regression

  • Coefficients on the log-odds scale with standard errors, z and p values.
  • Odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals, so the effect size is reported, not only its significance.
  • McFadden's pseudo-R² and AIC/BIC for the fitted model.
  • A classification table at the 0.5 threshold, with accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV.
  • A ROC curve with its AUC and a DeLong confidence interval, in a window you can edit and export.
  • A quality guard: perfect separation or a constant predictor produces a clear error naming the cause, never a table of invalid estimates.
How it works

Outcome and predictors, then the odds ratios

  1. Load the data. a binary outcome column and numeric predictors.
  2. Choose Logistic Regression. Statistics → Regression Models → Multinomial & Logistic Regression (binary outcome).
  3. Select the outcome and predictors. the dialog shows which level is treated as the event.
  4. Run it. coefficients, odds ratios, classification table and AUC appear together.
  5. Open the S-curve and the ROC. in their own windows for editing and export, then add the analysis to the report.
Options

The settings, in the dialog

Predictors: one or more numeric columns, selected together.

Frequently asked

Logistic regression questions, answered honestly

How is the outcome coded?

A 0/1 column is used directly, with 1 as the event. For a two-level text column, the levels are ordered and the second is treated as the event. The dialog prints the reference and the event level so the odds ratios are unambiguous.

What does an odds ratio mean?

An odds ratio above 1 means the odds of the event rise as the predictor rises; below 1, the odds fall. It is a multiplicative effect on the odds, not on the probability — for a common outcome the two differ substantially.

The model refused to fit. Why?

Almost always perfect separation or a constant predictor. When a predictor splits the outcome groups completely, the maximum-likelihood estimates do not exist and cannot be reported. Magic Stat identifies the offending predictor so you can remove it or reconsider the model.

How much data do I need?

Logistic regression needs enough events, not just enough rows — a handful of events against many predictors gives unstable estimates. Magic Stat requires at least 20 complete rows as a floor, but that is a minimum, not a target.

Do I need R?

No. The fit is a maximum-likelihood logistic model, and the AUC confidence interval uses the DeLong method. If you need penalised or mixed-effects logistic models, those live in other tools and in Magic Stat's other analyses.
