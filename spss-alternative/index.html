The honest part

Why people look for an SPSS alternative

The usual reasons are not mysterious: SPSS is expensive and licensed per module, the output window is awkward to turn into a publishable table, and the parts most researchers need (a clean figure, a table that survives Word, an interpretation) are exactly the parts it leaves to you.

There is also free software — R, JASP and jamovi — and if it suits you, use it. R is the reference implementation of half of modern statistics and JASP and jamovi are excellent, genuinely free, and open source. Magic Stat is not a replacement for them, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

So where does Magic Stat actually fit? The gap it aims at is the one between the analysis and the manuscript: getting the result into the article without an hour of formatting.

What it does

From your spreadsheet to a publishable figure

  • No code, no command line. Load the data, choose the test, read the result.
  • Tables already in APA convention. Mean (SD), CLD as real superscripts, exact p-values alongside the <.001*** column — and the table pastes into Word or LibreOffice intact, with column widths sized to the content.
  • Publication-quality figures. Boxplots, correlation matrices and the rest, with legends placed outside the plot and scientific colour palettes.
  • Method coverage. From t-tests to SEM/PLS, mixed models, GAM, meta-analysis, psychometrics, time series and Bayesian analysis — one application, one visual language, no per-module licence.
  • A report you did not have to write. Every analysis goes into an automatic report with the interpretation, the assumptions and the effect size, exportable to .docx, .html or .md.
  • An AI assistant that runs the same engines. It executes the same code paths as the interface, so what it reports and what the interface shows agree by construction.
  • Your data stays on your machine. Nothing leaves the computer unless you ask it to.
Frequently asked

Questions worth answering honestly

Is it free?

No. There is a free 7-day trial with no credit card, and after that the licence is US$99 per year. If you want free and open source, R, JASP and jamovi are excellent — Magic Stat competes on output and on the analysis-to-manuscript workflow, not on price.

Can it read the files I already have?

Yes — spreadsheets are the primary input, so you can keep the data where it is.

Do I have to send my data anywhere?

No. The analysis runs locally. The only automatic signal the app sends is an anonymous installation counter (no data, no names, no e-mail).

Does it replace SPSS entirely?

For most day-to-day work in a research group, probably yes. For a very specific procedure, check the method list first — if something is missing, that is useful to know and worth telling me about.

Which systems?

Windows, macOS and Linux.
