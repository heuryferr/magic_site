The honest part

Why this fits an analysis company better than it fits an individual researcher

Three things decide whether an analysis contract is profitable: how long it takes, whether the deliverable looks the same regardless of who ran it, and whether you can sign the NDA the client sends without adding a data-protection review.

Magic Stat is aimed at the first two and solves the third by architecture: the analysis runs on the machine. There is no upload and no cloud processing, so client data never goes anywhere — there is nothing to declare beyond what you already do with any local software.

It is honest to say where this does not fit. If your team already has a solid R pipeline and a statistician who maintains it, keep it — that is a real asset. Magic Stat is for the teams where the bottleneck is that only one person can run the analysis, or where the hours after the analysis (formatting the table, rebuilding the figure) are what eats the margin.

What it does

What it gives a team that sells analysis

  • Client data never leaves the machine. No upload, no cloud, nothing to add to a data-protection review — which is usually the first question a client asks when the data is theirs.
  • No code, so the whole team delivers. Analysts who do not program run regression, mixed models, SEM/PLS, ordination, meta-analysis and time series. The work stops depending on one R expert being available.
  • Consistent deliverables, whoever runs it. The same figures and the same tables come out in the same format, so the client cannot tell which analyst did the job.
  • The report writes itself. Every analysis goes into a report with the method, the settings, the assumptions and the interpretation — exportable to .docx, .html or .md for the client.
  • Every method under one licence. No per-module licences, no package dependency to maintain, no version drift between analysts.
  • macOS, Windows and Linux. A mixed team is not a problem.

Frequently asked

Questions a company actually asks

Can you issue an invoice?

Yes. Licences are invoiced annually. Send the billing entity, how many licences you need, and any field your purchasing process requires — the quote comes back with those filled in.

Do we have to send the client data to you?

No — and that is the point. There is nothing to send: the analysis runs on your machine. The only signal the software sends automatically is an anonymous installation counter, with no data in it.

How does licensing work for a team?

One licence activates on 2 computers. A larger team simply buys more licences — tell us how many and we send the quote. If your team shares machines or rotates laptops, that is worth counting before you order.

Is there support?

Email support from the person who builds it. For team licences we also answer method questions about your own data, within reason — that is usually faster than a forum.

We already work in R. Why would we change?

If R works for your team, it is a real asset and you should keep it. The case for Magic Stat is narrower and practical: work that currently queues behind one person, and the formatting hours after the analysis.

Can we use it with data under NDA?

The software runs locally and does not transmit the data. Whether that satisfies a specific contract is a question for your client and your legal review — but there is no upload and no cloud processing on our side.
